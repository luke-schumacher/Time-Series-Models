Examination duration model · standing after the 2026-08-21 checkpoint

Two minutes or twenty?

Georg is right that an MRI examination runs about twenty minutes, and right that something is off — but not the thing he suspected. Our 2 minutes is per scan, and per scan we are accurate. Aggregate to the examination, which is what he meant, and the synthetic day comes out ~30% short — because the generator puts no time at all between one scan and the next. That gap is now sampled from the real distribution, which brings the examination back to 21.2 minutes against a real 21.7.

The measurement

Both numbers were correct. Only the label was wrong.

The step-07 report has a section headed EXAMINATION DURATION that reads mean: 1.9 minutes. One row of the exam CSV is one scan — a single MSR_100 → MSR_104 span — not one examination. A radiologist's "examination" is the patient's whole visit: every scan they receive, plus the dead time between those scans.

Measured on the ten real scanners we hold locally (41,106 scans, 4,747 visits, January 2024):

Root cause

The clock only ever advances by the scan.

In 07_generate_synthetic_data.py, each generated scan returns t = msr_start_t + duration_sec, and the per-patient loop feeds that straight back in as the next scan's start. Scan N+1 therefore begins at the exact instant scan N ends. Nothing in the generator ever models the pause between two sequences of the same examination.

This is not a duration-model error. The examination model is trained on, and only responsible for, the MSR_100 → MSR_104 span. The gap belongs to the generator, the same way the patient-handover exchange block does — and the handover is modelled, which is why the number looks fine between patients and vanishes inside them.

Level 3 is the one that matters, and it is the whole point of this fork

Permutation importance over 272 inputs returns two live features and 270 dead ones: protocol at 60.9 s and sequence_type at 19.7 s. Every SUT parameter, plus body_region and serial_idx, sits at 0.0 s. Perturbing all 267 parameters threefold moves the prediction by 0.000 s (max 0.003 s). The model is a protocol lookup table — and, at 17.8 s against the lookup table's 16.2 s, a slightly worse one.

That is not because the parameters lack signal. On the same data, step 03e's tabular model over 89 admissible parameters reaches 9.7 s MAE, beating the protocol oracle's 13.2 s. Roughly 8 seconds of MAE is sitting on the table, unused by the network.

And the prescribed fix has already been tried

Step 06 has been printing one remedy for this: inject the conditioning token per-position into the duration encoder, mirroring duration_seq_type_bias. That code exists. duration_cond_bias is in sequence_generator.py, and the file's hash in the training manifest (ecf0d7a…) matches the current commit — so this checkpoint was trained with it, and the channel is still dead.

So it is no longer a wiring problem, and retraining the same objective will not fix it. The leading explanation is credit assignment: duration_protocol_bias is a direct 1,620-row per-position lookup that already explains 76% of held-out variance, so the gradient reaching the 277-dimensional conditioning path dies early. Step 06 now prints ‖W‖ for that layer, which settles it on the next run without another training cycle.

Next

Ranked by how much each moves Georg's number.

  1. Put the gap back between scans done AlternatingPipeline/data/intra_visit_gap.py · 17 tests

    The pause is now drawn per scan from a 101-point empirical quantile grid built at startup from the real exam CSVs, keyed on the sequence type it precedes — the pause before a scout is a stage change and runs 5–10× longer than the rest (median 168 s vs 19 s), which a single mean or a fitted lognormal would erase, along with the 3% of gaps that are genuinely zero. Simulated end to end against the real prior: examination wall clock 14.1 → 21.2 min (real 21.7), dead-time share 0 → 33.9% (real 30.4%).

    Watch on the next run: days are now ~30% longer in their exam portion and will push harder against the 13-hour budget that already truncates four scanners. If truncation gets worse, the scans-per-visit draw — currently Poisson(8) against a real 8.6 with a shorter tail — is the thing to adjust, not the gap.

  2. Settle the dead-conditioning question with one number one cell · step 06

    Run the new diagnostic before spending another training cycle. If ‖duration_cond_bias‖ ≈ 0, the parameter never trained and a retrain is the fix. If it is non-zero — which is what I expect — the wiring is fine and the objective is the problem, so go to step 3 and do not retrain the same loss again.

  3. Take the protocol shortcut out of the target, not the model the real experiment

    Train the duration head on the residual against the protocol mean — log(duration) − log(protocol_mean) — or run a straight ablation with num_protocols=None. Either removes the cheap route to the loss and makes the parameters the only way to reduce it. Score against the protocol oracle at 16.2 s, per Görtler; the tabular model's 9.7 s is the stretch target.

    This is the experiment that tells us whether the network can use sequence parameters at all, which is the question this entire fork exists to answer.

  4. Decide the fork explicitly: one checkpoint is being asked to be two models

    The generative day-simulator wants protocol as an input — it is what makes the durations realistic, and that was a deliberate call. The transferable duration model must not have it, because customers rename and copy protocols freely and a protocol-keyed model transfers to no one. Those are two models with two feature sets. Right now one checkpoint is doing the first job and failing the second, and every "the parameters do nothing" result is really a result about that conflict.

  5. Bring the abort rate down from 26.4% to ~3%

    Training oversamples MRI_MSR_34 sequences 2× (1,640 duplicated into 49,005), which puts aborts at ~6.7% of the training distribution — not enough on its own to explain 26.4%, so the decoder is over-generating the abort token beyond what oversampling accounts for. Worth confirming against the token-level validation perplexity before changing the sampler.

  6. Retrain exchange and orchestration on the seqparams pkl

    Both are fifteen days old and were fitted on the 67 MB csv_pipeline pkl while examination now runs on the 414 MB seqparams one. That mismatch is also why step 06 could not load a baseline, leaving criteria 2 and 3 with no comparison at all.
